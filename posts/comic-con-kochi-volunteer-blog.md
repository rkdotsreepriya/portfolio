#  Volunteering at Comic Con Kochi: A Walkie-Talkie and a Whole New World

"Why do you want to volunteer?"

I got asked this a lot in the weeks leading up to Comic Con Kochi 2026. Friends asked. Family asked. Even I asked myself this at one point, standing in a queue for a volunteer interview I had signed up for on a whim.

I didn't have a neat answer ready. I still don't, really. But I'll try to explain it here, because writing this down feels like the only way to make sense of what turned out to be one of the more memorable weekends I've had in a while.

## The Box I Wanted to Step Out Of

For the last four years, my life has revolved almost entirely around preschool education. Curriculum development, teacher training, children, more curriculum development. It's work I care about, and I don't say any of this to complain. But somewhere along the way, I noticed I hadn't done anything outside that world in a long time.

I wasn't looking for an escape. I want to be clear about that. I just wanted to see what else was out there, even for a couple of days.

I'm not a die-hard comics person. I haven't read every Marvel arc or debated the multiverse timeline over chai. But I'd been to Kerala Pop Con and Indie Comic Fest in previous years, and what pulled me in wasn't really the comics themselves. It was everything around them — the cosplay, the artists hunched over their tables, the strangers bonding instantly over a shared fictional universe, the sheer energy of a room full of people who are genuinely excited to be there.

So when I heard Kerala was finally getting its own full-scale Comic Con, I didn't want to just walk around as a visitor again. I wanted to see how the whole thing actually came together. I applied to volunteer.

## An Interview, and an Unexpected Promotion

I got a call for an interview, which already felt like a bigger deal than I expected for a volunteering gig. After the interview, I was told I'd be Team Lead for Stage Management.

I'll admit, stage management wasn't entirely unfamiliar territory. I'd previously worked as a Program Coordinator at Make My Events & Weddings, so I've spent my share of time around cue sheets and mic checks and things going wrong five minutes before showtime. Comic Con itself was new. Managing a stage wasn't.

Still, "Team Lead" is a very different phrase than "coordinator," and I remember thinking: okay, let's see what this actually means.

![My team](assets/wanda_mascot.jpg)
## The Meetup, and How I Became "The Witch"

We had a volunteer meetup before the event, held at the Entri office — Rahul Ramesh, the CTO there, was kind enough to let us use the space. That's where I met most of my team for the first time.

There were about sixteen of us in the stage team. I was the only woman leading a team, which I noticed but didn't think too much about at the time.

What I did not expect was that within an hour of meeting each other, someone decided our team needed a mascot, and the mascot needed to be a Marvel character. They picked Wanda Maximoff. The Witch.

Somehow, by association, I became "The Witch" too.

I've been called plenty of things in various jobs over the years. Coordinator. Ma'am. "The person who fixes the mic." But "The Witch" was definitely a first. What a wonderful way to start my Comic Con journey.

![At Comicon with my team](assets/team.jpg)
## The Team

I'd known this group for all of a few hours, and yet they made me feel completely comfortable, almost immediately. There was a kind of easy, brotherly energy to the whole team — some of them were experienced volunteers who'd done this kind of thing before, others were doing it for the very first time, wide-eyed and slightly nervous, much like me.

Nobody made a big deal out of the fact that I was new to this specific world. Everyone just pitched in, cracked jokes, and got to work. It's rare to find a group that's simultaneously that supportive and that unserious about itself. I got lucky with mine.

## Two Days I Barely Remember, Except the Stage

Here's the honest truth: I remember almost nothing about the two event days outside the stage area. Not the food stalls, not the merchandise tables, not most of the panels happening elsewhere. My entire world for those two days was the stage, the wings, and the console.

The walkie-talkie never stopped.

"Guest is running fifteen minutes late."
"Schedule's shifting, can you hold the next session?"
"The presentation file isn't rendering, we need five minutes."
"Backstage needs you, now."

It was one thing after another, all day, both days. My team kept running — literally running — between the console and the stage, solving problems as they came, without complaining once. Someone would sprint off to sort out a laptop issue, someone else would be calming down a guest who'd just landed and had zero time to prep, and somewhere in the middle of it I'd be trying to keep the schedule from collapsing entirely.

It was hectic. It was exhausting. My feet hurt in ways I didn't know feet could hurt.

And somehow, I enjoyed almost every minute of it.

## Seeing the Other Side of the Fandom

Between the chaos, I got to meet some of the people behind Kerala Pop Con and several of the Marvel and fan communities I'd only ever seen from the audience side before. Talking to them, even briefly, gave me a completely different sense of what goes into building these events and communities.

It's easy to walk into a con as a visitor and just enjoy the spectacle of it. It's a different thing entirely to see the planning, the last-minute scrambling, the sheer stubbornness it takes to pull something like this off, especially for the first time in a new city. I have a lot more respect for fandom events now than I did before I signed up.

## What I Actually Took Away From This

I don't think I learned any grand life lesson from volunteering at Comic Con Kochi. I didn't come away with a five-point plan for anything.

What I did get was a reminder that it's worth stepping outside your usual world every once in a while, even when you don't have a strong reason for it. Sometimes you don't volunteer because you're an expert at something. Sometimes you do it because you're curious. Because you want to meet new people, learn how something works from the inside, or just experience a couple of days that look nothing like your regular routine.

If the organizers ever need a hand for future comic events in Kerala, I'd sign up again without thinking twice. Walkie-talkie, chaos, and all.

I'm already looking forward to the next...
