**The Movie Showed Me the World. The Book Answered My Questions.**

I have a bit of a backward reading habit.

I’m not usually much of a fiction reader. My bookshelf mostly sits quiet unless a very specific mood strikes. But every now and then, I’ll watch a movie, fall completely into its world, and immediately go buy the book. I’ve done this with *Harry Potter*, *Twilight*, *The Notebook*, *A Walk to Remember*, and even the *Demon Slayer* manga. There’s just something satisfying about knowing the landscape before you walk through it.

Which brings me to *Project Hail Mary*.

![Project Hail Mary](/assets/movies_book.jpg)

I recently watched the film adaptation starring Ryan Gosling, and I was completely hooked. Leaving the theater, I felt that familiar itch, the curiosity to see how the story originally existed on the page. How faithfully had it been translated? What got left on the cutting room floor? Would reading it actually add anything new, or had I already gotten the best version?

(No spoilers here, by the way. I promise.)

I picked up the book shortly after. It sits at roughly 500 pages, and I flew through it in five days. For someone who doesn’t read fiction regularly, that rate of reading says everything. I found myself looking forward to every free pocket of time in my day just to read the next chapter.

What surprised me most was how much I loved the dynamic between the main astronaut and the alien. On screen, it’s entertaining, but on the page, their connection is wonderful. It’s funny, unexpected, and surprisingly warm. Watching two entirely different beings learn how to communicate, build trust, and solve problems together became my favorite part of the entire experience.

Then, there’s the science. I’m certainly not a science-heavy reader, and I definitely didn't grasp every single technical detail on the first try. But Andy Weir writes science in a way that never feels like a lecture or empty background noise. It makes you curious. Every time a question popped into my head *Wait, why does that work?* the book seemed to have an answer waiting a few lines down.

![Project Hail Mary](/assets/book.jpg)


That’s really where the book excels. A two-hour movie simply doesn't have room for every piece of logic, trial, and error. The film gave me the story and the visual spark and the book gave me the *why*.

I don't look at this as a *the book was better than the movie* situation. They do two different things really well. The film is a remarkable achievement, translating a heavy, highly technical narrative into something visual and engaging. But the book gives you space. It lets you sit with the reasoning, the quiet moments of problem-solving, and the deepening bond between the characters.

Honestly, watching the film first made reading the book even better. I already had a visual framework for the ships, the space, and the faces, which left my mind free to just enjoy the details and fill in the gaps.

If you enjoy a good story, I can’t recommend experiencing both enough. The movie made me love the world. The book made me appreciate everything it took to build it.
